3. You are using AI, and AI can be wrong

Explanations, flashcards and quiz questions generated by AI are produced automatically by a language model. You are interacting with an AI system, not a person, and its output is not reviewed by any person, tutor or editor. AI models can and do produce content that is incorrect, incomplete, outdated, biased, or entirely made up, including invented facts, dates, names, numbers, formulas, quotes and sources. Text recognized from your photos or handwriting can also be misread. You are solely responsible for checking anything you learn from AI output against your class materials or other reliable sources before relying on it.

The assistant is an AI, not a person. The chat assistant (18 and over) can misunderstand you, get facts or dates wrong, or suggest cards, tasks or exams that don't match your class. Check every suggestion before you tap to add it, and check dates against your syllabus. It is not a counselor; if you're struggling, talk to someone you trust, and in the US you can call or text 988 at any time.

Simple explanations leave things out. Explain it simply and other plain words explanations trade detail for clarity on purpose. Their examples, comparisons and memory hooks are rough pictures, not exact definitions, and a test may expect the precise wording or the parts they skip.

Language help can be wrong. AI verb tables, conjugations, example sentences and translations can contain mistakes. Speech recognition used by the pronunciation check can mishear you or understand a word you said wrong, so a high match doesn't prove your pronunciation is correct and a low one doesn't prove it's wrong.

Voice and accessibility features have limits. Never use hands free study or a spoken quiz while driving, cycling or doing anything that needs your attention. Hands free study can mishear an answer or a rating and save the wrong one. Fonts made for readers with dyslexia, extra spacing and color blind safe colors help some people and not others; they don't diagnose, treat or replace support from a school or specialist.

Shared and imported content can be wrong. Decks from other people, Quizlet exports, Word documents and PDFs (especially scanned pages read by text recognition) can contain mistakes or be split into cards the wrong way. Check imported cards before relying on them. Leaderboards and Study together scores are simple counts; they don't measure what you know.

Text the App reads for you can be wrong too. Text read from handwriting, photos and voice memos, cards suggested from your notes, and study guides built from them are made automatically and can contain mistakes or leave things out. Check them against your own notes before you rely on them.

Schools, classes and test providers have different rules about AI. Some do not allow AI help on homework, essays, take-home tests or exams. You are responsible for knowing and following those rules. Do not submit AI output as your own work where that is not allowed, and never use the App during a test or to copy, reconstruct or share secure exam content.

Practice test grades, explain-it-back scores, spoken quiz results, pronunciation match percentages, verb drill scores, study plans, exam pacing, cram sessions, eased new card limits, tough card flags and study habit patterns are estimates made by simple formulas from your own data; they can be wrong, and following them does not mean you are ready for a test. Course content and exam formats change, and the App's output may not match your class or your exam.
