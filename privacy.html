Nightfloat Privacy Policy

Version 2026.11.05.2 · Effective November 5, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains what information the Nightfloat app ("App") handles, how it is used, and the choices you have. It is written for users in the United States. Damian Cedeno ("we," "us") is responsible for any personal information described here. If you do not agree with this policy, do not use the App. If you are under 18, please read this policy with your parent or guardian.

1. The short version

  • Nightfloat is for students 13 and older. Users under 18 need a parent or guardian's permission. The App is not directed to children under 13 and does not continue for anyone who enters a birth date under 13.
  • Your study data (decks, cards, notes, progress, exams, saved explanations, photos you keep) is stored on your device, or, if you choose it, in your own iCloud account via Apple's CloudKit. We do not provide account registration or a Nightfloat login. Your profile is stored locally (and profile information may be included in your iCloud data if enabled). Your study data is not uploaded to our own user-account servers, though content you deliberately submit for cloud AI is processed as described in Section 3.
  • No registration or Nightfloat login is required. You can create a local personal profile with a name or nickname and optional photo; the profile is not a Nightfloat account.
  • Your birth date stays on your device. It is never uploaded.
  • AI features send the material you ask about to an AI model. Depending on your settings that is on your device (nothing leaves the phone) or a cloud model (the text and, if enabled, the photo are transmitted to Anthropic, directly or through our proxy server).
  • We do not sell personal information, do not show ads, do not use advertising trackers, and do not build profiles about you.
  • Don't put sensitive personal information (passwords, ID numbers, health information, or private information about other people) in the App.

2. Information the App handles

a. Study content you create. Decks, cards of every type (including back to front cards the App makes for you when you turn that on, the items of list and order cards, the pictures and covered areas of image occlusion cards, and audio you record for a card), hints, tags, review history (each rating, and if you answer them, why you missed a card and how sure you felt before flipping), exam dates, notes and the decks you link to each exam, typed text, ink drawings, photos, imported decks, saved explanations and AI output. Stored in the App's database on your device and included in your device backups (iCloud or computer) according to your iOS settings.

b. Where your data is stored: your choice. On first launch you choose device-only or iCloud. Device-only: the database stays in the App's container on your device (and in any device backup you make). iCloud: the same data is synced by Apple's CloudKit to the private database of your iCloud account and to your other devices signed in with the same Apple Account. We have no access to your iCloud private database; it is governed by Apple's iCloud terms and privacy policy. You can switch modes in Settings (applies at next launch). We are not responsible for data loss in either mode; keep your own backups.

c. Mastery records and quiz history. The App keeps per-topic counts of your quiz answers and flashcard ratings (for example "Photosynthesis: 7 of 10 correct") and a history of finished quizzes so you can see what needs practice and retake quizzes. It also shows patterns from your own review history: which cards you keep missing, the reasons you gave for misses, and how often you were right when you felt sure. These are study data stored with the rest of your data and are never sent to us.

d. Notes. The text you type (with its formatting), ink drawings, photos, PDF pages and voice memos in a note are study content stored with the rest of your data. Notes made in an older version are converted to the new format the first time they open, on your device. Text read from note photos or handwriting is stored with the note. Nothing in Notes is sent anywhere unless you choose an AI action on a note, in which case the note's text is processed as described in Section 3.

e. Photos and camera. When you take or choose a photo, the App reads text from it on your device using Apple's Vision framework (also used by Find labels when you make an image occlusion card). The photo is not sent anywhere unless you use a cloud AI engine (see Section 3b) or choose to keep it in your study data.

f. Calendar and reminders. If you turn on "Add to iOS Calendar," the App asks for calendar permission and then creates an event in your default calendar for each exam you save (title, course, date and your notes), with reminders. It stores the event identifier so it can update or remove that event. The App does not copy or transmit your other calendar events. If you turn on "Show my iOS Calendar events on the home hub," the App reads the titles and times of your events for the next 7 days to display them on screen only; they are not stored and never transmitted. Reminders are local notifications scheduled on your device. You can revoke calendar or notification access at any time in iOS Settings.

g. Your birth date. Before setup the App asks for your birth date. It is used only to confirm you are old enough to use the App and to apply the protections for users under 18 described in Section 9. It is stored in the App's preferences on your device only and is never uploaded. If the date shows you are under 13, the App does not store the date or anything else you entered; it keeps only a flag on the device so that setup does not continue.

h. Settings. What you're studying for, the optional class, subject or exam you type, your AI engine choice, new-card limit, reminder times and proxy server address are stored in the App's preferences on your device. Adults can enter a different proxy server address in Advanced AI settings; AI requests then go to that server instead of the Nightfloat proxy, what this policy says about the Nightfloat proxy does not apply to it, and that choice is yours. Users under 18 can't change the address. A Claude API key, if you enter one (adults only), is stored in the iOS Keychain on your device and sent only to api.anthropic.com with your requests. When the Nightfloat proxy is used, the App creates a random identifier (not tied to you or your Apple Account), keeps it in the iOS Keychain and sends it with each proxy request so we can rate-limit per device.

h-1. App Lock. If you turn on App Lock, iOS checks your Face ID, Touch ID, Optic ID or passcode and tells the App only whether it unlocked. The App never receives, stores or transmits biometric data.

h-2. Backups. A backup file contains all your study data (including photos, notes, ink, progress and quiz history). Your birth date, API key and proxy identifier are never included, and backups are never sent to us.

  • Saved backup files are handed to iOS so you can save them where you choose (for example iCloud Drive, another app or a computer). You can protect a file with a password; it is then encrypted on your device with AES-256 using a key made from your password. We never receive or store the password and cannot recover it. Without a password the file is readable by anyone who has it.
  • Automatic and on-device backups (if turned on, weekly by default) are saved inside the App's own folder on your device, visible in the Files app under On My iPhone or iPad → Nightfloat → Backups, and are included in your device backups according to your iOS settings. The App also saves one here before "Replace everything" restores a file, and when you choose "Back up first" before a reset or before deleting your data. The newest four automatic backups are kept; the others stay until you delete them. They are protected by your device passcode, not by a separate password. "Delete all my data" can remove them too, and deleting the App deletes them. You are responsible for where you keep backup files and who can open them.

h-3. Read aloud. If you use read aloud, the text of the card is spoken by the voices built into iOS on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere.

h-3b. Microphone and recordings. The App asks for microphone access only when you tap Record to add audio to a card, record a voice memo on a note page, start a spoken quiz or hands free study (see h-3d) or check your pronunciation (see h-3j). It records only while you're recording (30 seconds at most for a card, 10 minutes at most for a voice memo). A card's recording is saved with that card, and a voice memo with its note, as study content (on your device, and in your iCloud if you use iCloud mode). Recordings are included in backups (and card recordings in deck files you export), and are never sent to us or to any AI service. Voice memos are for your own voice only; before the first one the App asks you to agree not to record other people. You can delete a recording in the card editor or by deleting the memo from the page, and turn off microphone access in iOS Settings at any time.

h-3d. Speech recognition (spoken quiz, hands free study and voice memos). During a spoken quiz or hands free study the App listens through the microphone only while it's waiting for your answer or your rating, and turns what you say into text with Apple's speech recognition. Hands free study only listens while the App is open and on screen (it pauses when you leave the App), and it keeps the screen on while it runs. When you tap Turn into text on a voice memo, that memo is turned into text the same way. On devices that support it, this happens on your device; on other devices Apple may process the audio on its servers under Apple's privacy policy. In a spoken quiz the App doesn't keep the audio or the text of what you said; only right or wrong is recorded (in quiz history and Mastery). In hands free study it doesn't keep the audio or the text either; only the rating you gave each card is saved, the same as when you tap it. A voice memo's text is added to the note as a text box, which you can edit or delete. You can turn off microphone or speech recognition access in iOS Settings at any time.

h-3r. Settings added in version 1.21. The hour your study day starts, spacing and card timer choices, haptics and sounds, quiet hours and your app icon choice are preferences stored on your device. "Export everything as files" makes a zip of your own content (deck spreadsheets, note PDFs and text, tasks, exams, grades, schedule, quiz and focus history) in a temporary folder and hands it to the iOS share sheet; you choose where it goes, it never includes your birthday, keys or settings, and it is deleted from the temporary folder when you close that screen. "Storage on this device" only measures what the App already stores; nothing is sent anywhere.

h-3l. Accessibility settings. Your reading font, letter and line spacing, color choice and hands free settings are saved on your device only. The reading fonts are built into the App and nothing is downloaded. The App reads iOS accessibility settings you've turned on (such as text size, Bold Text, Reduce Motion and Differentiate Without Color) only to adjust how it looks, on your device; it doesn't record or send them.

h-3m. Sharing decks. When you share a deck, the App makes a file with the deck's cards (and, if you choose, its pictures, recordings and example sentences) and hands it to the iOS share sheet; you pick who gets it (AirDrop, Messages, Mail or another app). Your progress, study history and notes are never included. The file goes directly to the people you choose through Apple's or the other app's service; it is not sent to us. Anyone who gets the file can keep or forward it, so only share with people you trust. The deck and each card carry a random id so a newer copy can update the recipient's deck. The file is signed with a key the App makes on your device (kept in a protected file on that device only, not in iCloud or backups, and deleted by "Delete all my data"); it contains the matching public key, so only a newer copy from your device can update a friend's copy. Unsigned or changed files are added as new decks and can't update anything.

h-3s. Your local profile, name, and sharing notes. During setup the App asks what to call you (a first name or nickname is fine). Your name is stored on your device (and in your iCloud if you use iCloud storage) and is never sent to us. It's included only in things you choose to share: a shared deck, note or folder says who it's from, and Study together and the friends board start with it. You can change or remove it any time in Settings → You, and "Delete all my data" removes it. When you share a note or folder, the App makes a file with its text, formatting and, if you choose, its photos, handwriting and voice memos (voice memos are off unless you turn them on), and you pick who gets it through the iOS share sheet. Writing you share from the App (an explanation, essay, cheat sheet or lesson) is sent the same way, as a note, a PDF or text. Notes someone shares with you keep the sender's name so you know who they're from.

h-3n. Opening files and importing documents. When you open a file in Nightfloat (a shared deck or note, a friend's week, an Anki, CSV or JSON deck, a Quizlet export you paste, a Word document, a text file or a PDF), it's read on your device. Scanned PDF pages are read with Apple's text recognition on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you then choose "Make cards with AI", which sends the text as described in Section 3. From Files (next to the photo buttons in Scan & explain, Hint mode, the syllabus scanner, card pictures, image occlusion and note pages) opens Apple's Files picker; the App can read only the files you pick there, only at that moment, and reads them on your device. A picture you pick is treated exactly like a photo from your library (its text is read on your device, and it's saved or sent only the way that feature handles photos); text from a PDF or document is put in the text box, and is sent to AI only when you tap an AI button, as described in Section 3. Material you scan, paste or type (a syllabus, say) can include other people's details, such as an instructor's name or email address; when you use an AI feature on it, that text goes to the AI provider along with the rest, so leave out what isn't needed. Files from other people can contain mistakes or things you don't want; only open files from people you know.

h-3o. Study together. When you host or join a Study together game, the App uses your local network and peer-to-peer Wi-Fi to find phones nearby, after asking for Local Network permission. It shares only the nickname you type, the questions (text from the host's chosen deck) and everyone's answers and scores, and only with phones that entered the game's join code and that the host let in; the game's messages are encrypted with a key made from that code. This is meant to keep a game among friends in the same room, not to protect secret information, so don't put anything private in a deck you use for a game. Nothing goes through our servers. When the game ends, nothing about the other players is kept; your own result is saved like a quiz on your device.

h-3p. Friends board. If you send your week, the App makes a small file with the name you type, a board id and five numbers for the last 7 days: cards reviewed, days studied, streak, focus minutes and badges. The file is signed with your device's sharing key (see h-3m), and your board id is a short fingerprint of that key's public part, so it isn't tied to your name, account or device and no one else can send a week as you. You choose who gets it. A week that isn't signed by the phone it claims to come from is refused. Weeks friends send you are kept in a file on your device only (not in iCloud, device backups or Nightfloat backups) and removed after 30 days, when you remove them, or with "Delete all my data," which also forgets your board name and deletes the sharing key, so you get a new board id.

h-3j. Pronunciation check. When you tap the microphone on a card (or use Pronunciation practice), the App listens only until you stop talking (10 seconds at most) and turns what you said into text with Apple's speech recognition on your device only. If your device can't recognize that language offline, the App tells you and doesn't listen; it never sends this audio to Apple's servers, to us or to any AI service. The audio isn't recorded or saved, and the text of what you said is shown on screen and then discarded; nothing about your pronunciation is kept.

h-3k. Verb drills and example sentences. Verb drills check the forms you type on your device. The first time you drill a verb, its name, the language and the tense are sent to the AI engine you chose (Section 3) to write its forms, and the result is saved in a file on your device only (not in iCloud, not in iCloud or computer backups of your device, and not in Nightfloat backups) so later drills work offline; "Delete all my data" and deleting the App remove it, and you can delete saved tables in Verb drills → Saved verb tables. Example sentences are written by AI only when you ask, from the card's question and answer (and the deck's language); they're saved on the card like the rest of your study content and included in backups and in Nightfloat deck files you export.

h-3e. Handwriting search. When Search my handwriting is on (it is unless you turn it off in Settings → Notes), the App reads the handwriting on your note pages with Apple's text recognition, on your device, and keeps the text it reads and where each word is with the note (on your device, and in your iCloud if you use iCloud mode) so search and Find in note can find it. Nothing is sent to us or to any AI service. Turning the setting off deletes the text already read; backups don't include it, because it's read again on the device.

h-3f. Widgets and Live Activities. If you add Nightfloat widgets, the App writes a small summary (how many cards are due, your streak, your next few exams, today's tasks and your class times for the coming week) to a folder on your device that only Nightfloat and its widgets can read, so the widgets can show it on your Home Screen and Lock Screen. While a focus timer runs, the time left shows on your Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. Anyone who can see your screen can see what the widgets show. With App Lock on and "hide exam names" on (Privacy & security), widgets show "Your exam" and "Class" instead of names. The Review cards widget also gets the text of up to 20 cards that are due (300 characters each at most) so you can review them on your Home Screen, and keeps the ratings you tap there in the same folder until the App applies them; with App Lock and "hide exam names" on, it gets no card text at all. Nothing is sent to us.

h-3g. Siri and Shortcuts. You can ask Siri to study your due cards, add a task, say how many cards are due, start a focus timer or tell you your next class. Apple processes what you say to Siri under Apple's privacy policy; the App only receives the request (for example, the task's name) and handles it on your device. Nothing is sent to us.

h-3h. Apple Watch. If you install Nightfloat on your Apple Watch, your iPhone sends it the text of up to 60 cards that are due, the due count and your streak, and the watch sends back your answers and focus timer time. This goes directly between your paired iPhone and watch using Apple's WatchConnectivity; it isn't sent to us. The watch keeps the cards until new ones arrive; deleting the watch app removes them.

h-3i. Grades, class schedule, focus timer, badges and nudges. Grades, classes, your schedule and focus sessions are saved with your other study data (on your device, and in your iCloud if you use iCloud mode) and included in backups. Your streak, streak freezes, badges, heat map, weekly report, break suggestions and bedtime reminder are worked out on your device from your own study data. If you add Nightfloat's Focus filter to an iPhone Focus, iOS applies it to Nightfloat's notifications; the App doesn't learn which Focus you use. Class reminders and the weekly report reminder are local notifications you can turn off.

h-3c. Formulas, typed answers and games. Formulas are drawn on your device by a copy of KaTeX built into the App; nothing is loaded from the internet. Answers you type, the order you pick and the matching game are checked on your device; only your ratings and scores are kept (as review history, topic counts and the deck's best matching time).

h-3a. Fix with AI. If you tap Fix with AI on a card you keep missing, that card's question and answer are sent to the AI engine you chose, the same way as other AI features (Section 3b). Nothing changes until you pick the suggestions to keep.

h-3t. Optional AI answer quality feedback (adults only). In the AI assistant, you may rate an answer as helpful or not helpful and select an optional reason. Nightfloat creates a short preview containing only the rating and reason, without the prompt, AI response, chat history, study documents, profile or identifiers. Nothing is sent automatically. If you open the iOS share sheet, you choose whether and where to send the report. Your chosen service may share your contact information with the recipient and has its own privacy rules. Feedback received by Nightfloat is used to evaluate and improve AI answer quality. To request its deletion, contact dca.support@icloud.com with enough information to identify the report. The App does not silently collect ratings or use a feedback analytics SDK.

h-4. Support email. If you use "Contact support" or "Report a problem," the App opens your email app with a draft. A problem report includes your App version, iOS version and device model. Nothing is sent unless you send the email yourself, and no study data is attached.

i. Diagnostic data. The App does not include third-party analytics or crash-reporting SDKs. Apple may share aggregated crash and usage data with us through App Store Connect if you have enabled "Share with App Developers" in iOS settings; that data is governed by Apple's privacy policy.

j. Information we do not request for a developer account. We do not require your real name, email address, precise location, contacts, advertising identifier or health records to use the App. If you email support, we receive the address and information you choose to send. If you use cloud AI, our provider receives the submitted content; the proxy processes your device token and network IP address for operational rate limits. Your chosen nickname may be disclosed to peers when you use sharing and nearby study features.

3. AI processing

What each AI tutor tool sends. Nothing is sent until you tap the tool's button, and it goes to the AI engine you chose (on your device or the cloud model below): Explain it back sends the topic, your explanation and related text from your cards and notes; Hint mode sends your problem (and, with cloud AI, its photo) and the conversation so far; Essay coach sends the assignment, thesis and outline you type; Syllabus scanner sends the text read from your syllabus photos or from the syllabus file you pick; Practice tests send text from the deck, exam or note you pick and, for written answers, your answers for grading; Cheat sheet sends the note's text; Explain more in the mistake notebook sends that question; a spoken quiz with AI questions sends text from your cards; Write the question with AI (when you make a card from a note) sends the words you picked and the text box they're in; AI key points in a notebook study guide send each note's text, one note at a time; Example sentences send the question and answer of each card you pick (up to 10 cards per request) and the deck's language; Verb drills send the verbs you type, the language and the tense; Make cards with AI on an imported document sends its text; Explain it simply in a study session sends the card's question and answer and its deck's name (for Whole topic, text from the deck's cards, about 4,500 characters at most), and for Even simpler or More examples also what it already explained; Explain it simply on a note, cheat sheet or saved explanation sends that text (about 4,500 characters at most) and its title. The assistant (users 18 and over only) sends each message you type and, the first time and whenever they change, a short summary of your app (today's date, how many cards are due and new, your deck names, your next few exams with their dates and courses, and your open tasks with their due dates) and the text of the deck, note or saved explanation on screen when you opened it (about 2,500 characters at most). The assistant's chat is kept in memory only while the App is open and is cleared by New chat or by closing the App; it is never saved to your device, iCloud or backups. Things it offers to do (make cards, add a task or exam, start studying or a quiz, open a tab) happen only when you tap them. The pronunciation check uses no AI and sends nothing. The mistake notebook (questions you missed in quizzes) is stored with your other study data; the other tools' feedback is shown and not kept unless you save it (as cards, a note, exams, tasks or a saved explanation). Explain it simply lessons are kept in memory only until the study session ends, unless you tap Save to Tutor.

Automatic engine. With the engine set to Automatic, a request that the first engine can't complete (too much text, a refusal, a busy model, a lost connection, or an answer the App can't read) is sent to the next one in this order: the on-device model, Apple's cloud model (if available), then the Nightfloat cloud (if set up). When a request includes a photo and the Nightfloat cloud is set up, the Nightfloat cloud goes first, because it is the only engine that can see photos; a photo with no readable text is sent only there. In Hint mode and the assistant, a conversation that moves to the next engine takes a short recap with it (your first message and the latest few, with the replies), so it can carry on; your problem in Hint mode is also part of that engine's instructions. On the on-device model the App may also split the text into smaller parts and try again, and a long syllabus is read in parts (about 19,000 characters at most). Each part goes to the same engines as a whole request would. Choosing "On this device only" in Settings → AI keeps every request on the device.

a. On-device (Apple Intelligence). On supported devices with Apple Intelligence enabled, explanations, flashcards and quizzes can be generated by Apple's on-device model. Your material does not leave the device for this processing.

a2. Apple's cloud model (Private Cloud Compute). On Apple Intelligence devices running iOS 27 or later, when a request is too big for the on-device model, or that model declines it, the App can send the same text to Apple's larger model running on Apple's Private Cloud Compute servers, under Apple's privacy policy and the Apple Intelligence terms. Apple states that this data is used only to fulfil the request and is not stored or made accessible to Apple. We never receive it. Apple limits how much each user can send per day. You can turn this off in Settings → AI ("Apple's cloud model for bigger requests").

b. Cloud model (Claude by Anthropic). If you choose the cloud engine, or Automatic mode on a device without Apple Intelligence, the App transmits the text you entered or that was recognized from your photo, what you said you're studying for, and, if a photo is present, the downscaled photo itself, to Anthropic's API to generate a response. Transmission goes either (i) directly from your device using an API key you entered (adults only), or (ii) through a proxy server we operate ("Nightfloat proxy"), which forwards the request with our key.

  • With each proxy request the App sends a yes/no flag saying whether the user is under 18, so the proxy can add age-appropriate safety instructions. Your birth date and age are never sent.
  • Anthropic's handling of API data is described in its privacy and usage policies (anthropic.com/legal). As of the effective date, Anthropic states that API inputs and outputs are not used to train its models by default and are retained for a limited period for safety and abuse monitoring.
  • When the Nightfloat proxy is used, our server records the date, time, request size and response status, and uses pseudonymous hashes of your device token and network IP address, kept for up to 36 hours in rate-limit counters, to prevent abuse. The application code does not deliberately log the content of your requests or responses. Cloudflare and Anthropic necessarily process requests to provide the service; their infrastructure, security systems and applicable retention practices are outside our complete control. We cannot guarantee that no provider ever retains or accesses submitted content.
  • AI requests may include anything you typed or photographed. Do not include sensitive personal information or other people's private information.

4. How we use information

To operate the App's features you request; to apply age-appropriate protections; to prevent abuse and enforce rate limits on the proxy; to diagnose problems; and to comply with law. We do not use your study content for advertising, profiling, or training our own models.

5. Sharing

Files you choose to send to other people (shared decks, notes and folders, and your study week; see h-3m to h-3s) go straight from you to them; we're not involved and never receive them. We share information only with: (a) Anthropic, to process cloud AI requests you initiate; (b) Cloudflare, which hosts the proxy server and processes request metadata on our behalf under contract; (c) Apple, as part of App Store distribution, iOS features and, if you leave it on, Apple's cloud model (Section 3a2); and (d) authorities, if required by law, subpoena or to protect rights and safety. We do not sell or "share" (as defined by California law) personal information, and we do not disclose information about users under 18 for advertising or any purpose other than providing the App.

6. Retention and deletion

No account registration. Nightfloat offers a personal profile, but does not create a registered Nightfloat account. The Settings control called "Delete all my data" is a data reset, not account closure. This reset removes locally managed study content, profile name and photo, saved peer board information, and other selected app data. If you choose to make a backup before reset, the backup is kept until you delete it. Copies of shared files already received by others, exported documents in other apps, messages, and backups you stored outside Nightfloat cannot be recalled or deleted remotely. Cloud AI providers may retain request metadata or content according to their own retention policies. iCloud data and device backups may persist subject to Apple synchronization and backup behavior. For assistance with privacy or removal requests, email dca.support@icloud.com.

Study content stays on your device (and, in iCloud mode, in your iCloud private database) until you delete it in the App; deleting the App removes the local copy, and iCloud copies are removed when you delete them in the App or manage iCloud storage in iOS Settings. Your birth date is removed by "Delete all my data" or by deleting the App. Keychain entries (your API key, the proxy identifier) are removed when you clear the key in Settings, use "Delete all my data," or delete the App (Keychain data may persist in device backups per iOS behavior). Saved verb tables stay in a file on your device until you delete them, use "Delete all my data," or delete the App. Proxy rate-limit counters expire within approximately 36 hours. Operational log retention depends on the hosting provider account configuration; we will verify the active retention period before public release. Anthropic's retention is governed by its policies.

7. Security

Data on your device is protected by iOS device encryption and your passcode. Network requests use HTTPS/TLS. We limit what the proxy records, restrict access to it, and review these safeguards periodically. No method is perfectly secure; keep your device updated and locked.

8. Your choices and rights

We may not meet the revenue or data thresholds that make the California Consumer Privacy Act apply to us; we honor the requests below anyway, for everyone in the United States.

  • Turn off cloud AI: Settings → Engine → "On-device only." AI features will then work only on Apple Intelligence devices.
  • Remove your key: clear the API key field in Settings.
  • Reset Nightfloat data: Settings → Your data → "Delete all my data." If you choose a backup-first option, separately delete that backup when no longer needed. Uninstalling the App does not automatically delete copies in iCloud, exported files, other people's devices, or third-party service logs.
  • State privacy rights. Residents of California, Colorado, Connecticut, Virginia, Utah, Texas, Oregon, Montana and other states with consumer privacy laws may have rights to access, correct, delete or port personal information and to opt out of sale, sharing, targeted advertising or profiling. Because we hold almost no personal information on our servers, most requests can be satisfied by deleting the App; for anything else, email dca.support@icloud.com. A parent or guardian may make a request for a user under 18. We will not discriminate against you for exercising these rights. California residents: we do not sell or share personal information and have not done so in the preceding 12 months; we do not use or disclose sensitive personal information for purposes requiring a right to limit.

9. Children and teens

Children under 13. The App is not directed to children under 13. Before setup, the App asks for a birth date in a neutral way. If the date shows the user is under 13, the App stops, does not store the birth date or anything else entered, and does not transmit anything. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13, consistent with the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA). If you believe a child under 13 has provided personal information through the App, contact dca.support@icloud.com and we will delete anything we hold.
